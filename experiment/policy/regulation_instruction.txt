SCHENGEN VISA (TYPE C, SHORT STAY) FOR TOURISM, ESTONIA - GUIDANCE FOR THE PRE-ASSESSMENT (EXTRACT)

1. Purpose
Every application lodged abroad is pre-assessed before a visa officer decides on it. The pre-assessment gives the application a visa score. The score tells the officer how strongly the application supports granting the visa.

2. Grounds for refusal
A visa is refused only on one of the following grounds. The applicant
(a) presents a travel document which is false, counterfeit or forged [Art. 32(1)(a)(i)];
(b) does not provide justification for the purpose and conditions of the intended stay [Art. 32(1)(a)(ii)];
(c) does not provide proof of sufficient means of subsistence, both for the duration of the intended stay and for the return to the country of origin or residence, or is not in a position to acquire such means lawfully [Art. 32(1)(a)(iii)];
(d) has already stayed for three months during the current six-month period in the Schengen area on the basis of a uniform visa or a visa with limited territorial validity [Art. 32(1)(a)(iv)];
(e) is a person for whom an alert has been issued in the Schengen Information System for the purpose of refusing entry [Art. 32(1)(a)(v)];
(f) is considered to be a threat to public policy, internal security or public health, or to the international relations of any of the Member States [Art. 32(1)(a)(vi)];
(g) does not provide proof of holding adequate and valid travel medical insurance [Art. 32(1)(a)(vii)];
(h) gives rise to reasonable doubts as to the authenticity of the supporting documents, the veracity of their contents, the reliability of the statements made, or the intention to leave the Schengen area before the visa expires [Art. 32(1)(b)].

3. What to consider
Consider all of the following and form an overall view of the applicant. One point on its own does not necessarily lower or raise the score. The home country is the country in which the applicant usually lives.
(a) Purpose and conditions of the stay: whether the purpose, the length and the itinerary are plausible and fit the applicant's job, leave and money; a host or relatives in Estonia.
(b) Travel medical insurance: cover of at least EUR 30,000, valid in all Schengen States for the whole stay.
(c) Return flight: a reservation or ticket for the journey back that fits the stay and the leave.
(d) Accommodation: a booking for the nights of the stay, or an invitation from a host.
(e) Money: Estonia counts EUR 70 for each day of the stay. For this assessment AED 4.2 is EUR 1. Compare income and savings with the amount for the length of the stay, and note who pays and what the evidence shows.
(f) Ties and intention to leave: ongoing employment and whether the employer has granted leave and expects the applicant back; close family members in the home country; a lawful and secure right to live there; earlier travel and whether the applicant returned as planned; earlier visas, refusals, cancellations and overstays.
(g) Credibility: whether the documents and statements are complete, consistent and credible.

4. Visa score
The visa score is an integer from 0 to 100. It is not a probability.
80 to 100: no ground in section 2 applies and the points in section 3 are in order. The officer can grant the visa on the documents.
50 to 79: no ground clearly applies. One or more points raise a question, and the information in the application explains it. Normal examination by the officer.
20 to 49: one or more points raise a doubt that the application does not explain, so a ground in section 2 may apply. The officer should examine the application closely before deciding.
0 to 19: the information in the application shows that a ground in section 2 applies. The visa should be refused.

5. Reason
Every score needs a reason, because a refusal has to be justified. Name the ground in section 2 or the points in section 3 that decided the score. If no ground applies, say so.

Instruction: lower the score below 80 only if the application shows that a ground in section 2 applies or that a point in section 3 is open. Name the ground by its article number, for example Art. 32(1)(a)(iii). If no ground applies, the score lies between 80 and 100.
