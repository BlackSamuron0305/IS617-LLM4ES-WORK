VISITOR VISA (SUBCLASS 600), TOURIST STREAM - GUIDANCE FOR THE PRE-ASSESSMENT (EXTRACT)

1. Purpose
Every application lodged outside Australia is pre-assessed before a visa officer decides on it. The pre-assessment gives the application a risk rating. The rating decides how closely the application is examined.

2. Criteria for the visa
The applicant has to show that he or she
(a) genuinely intends to stay in Australia temporarily and for the purpose of the visa (a holiday, or a visit to family or friends), and to comply with the conditions of the visa, including leaving Australia before the visa ends;
(b) has, or has access to, adequate means to support himself or herself during the stay;
(c) meets the health and character requirements, and has given no false or misleading information or documents.

3. What to consider
Consider all of the following and form an overall view of the applicant. One point on its own does not necessarily put an application into a higher or a lower category. The home country is the country in which the applicant usually lives.
(a) Personal circumstances that would encourage the applicant to return to the home country at the end of the visit: ongoing employment, and whether the employer has granted leave and expects the applicant back; close family members there; a lawful and secure right to live there; a home, property or other commitments.
(b) Personal circumstances or general conditions in the home country that might encourage the applicant to remain in Australia: for example unemployment or a poor economic situation, war, lawlessness or political upheaval.
(c) The visit: whether the purpose, the length and the plans are plausible and fit the applicant's job, leave and money; relatives or friends in Australia.
(d) Money: income and savings in relation to the length and the cost of the stay; who pays for the visit; what the evidence shows.
(e) Travel and immigration history: earlier travel and whether the applicant returned as planned; earlier visas for Australia and for other countries; refusals, cancellations and overstays.
(f) Credibility: whether the documents and statements are complete, consistent and credible.

4. Rating
Low: nothing in the application raises doubt about the criteria. The officer can decide on the documents.
Medium: one or more points raise doubt, and the information in the application explains them. Normal examination by the officer.
High: several points raise doubt, or a point is not explained by the application. Further checks before a decision.

5. Further checks
A - request bank statements for the last six months
B - call the employer to confirm the job and the leave
C - request more evidence of ties to the home country (for example a tenancy agreement or family documents)
D - request a day-by-day itinerary and proof of bookings
E - interview the applicant
F - request a police certificate
Name only checks for which the application gives a reason.

6. Recommendation
grant: the criteria appear to be met; the officer can grant the visa on the documents.
refer: the officer should make the further checks named before deciding.
refuse: only if the information in the application shows that a criterion is not met.
