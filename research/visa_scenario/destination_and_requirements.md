# Visitor-visa scenario: which destination, and what the real application looks like

Research note, 2026-10-10. Written by Claude (research agent). Internal working
document, not report text. Every number has a source in the table at the end, with a
note on how far the source was read. Anything not checked is marked "not verified".

Reading codes used below: **R** = I read the raw page, PDF text or data file.
**S** = I read it only through the fetch tool's summary of the page. **Sn** = search
snippet only.

---

## 1. Short answer

**Australia is the only tested destination that meets all criteria.**

- All 22 Arab League nationalities need the same visa: **Visitor visa (subclass 600),
  Tourist stream**, applied for in advance.
- None of the 22 is on the list for the two light alternatives (ETA subclass 601,
  eVisitor subclass 651). Official grant data confirm it: in 2024-25 and 2025-26 not one
  ETA or eVisitor was granted to any of the 22 nationalities.
- No visa on arrival for them: Home Affairs says travellers "must have: a valid visa to
  enter Australia".
- No ban, pause or suspension applies to any of the 22 as of 10.10.2026, as far as I
  could verify. One nationality-based suspension did exist in 2026, for **Iranian**
  passports only, and it has ended (section 3.3).
- Same fee for all 22 (AUD 250), same online form, same legal test.

Every other tested destination fails, mostly because of the Gulf states: the UAE is
visa-free or on a light electronic permit almost everywhere.

Three things to know before building on it:

1. **The law still names nationality.** One criterion in force (public interest
   criterion 4011) lets the Minister list groups by nationality, age and sex as overstay
   risks. The list itself was revoked in 2009. It was replaced by internal risk profiles
   that are not published.
2. **Handling differs in practice.** The legal test is the same, but granted Gulf
   applications are decided in about a day at the median, Syrian and Palestinian ones
   in about a month.
3. **German citizens do not need this visa.** They use the free eVisitor. A German
   benchmark file would be a different visa, so it does not fit as a like-for-like
   benchmark.

Two extra points:

- **Schengen does not work for people living in Germany anyway.** A resident of Germany
  needs no visa to travel inside the Schengen area. (General rule, not verified here.)
- **Watch for residence-based exemptions.** Some countries waive the visa for anyone
  holding a Schengen residence permit. That would wipe out the manipulation for
  German residents. Australia has no such exemption: its rules go by passport only.
  Ireland states explicitly that a Schengen visa or residence card does not help.

## 3. Australia: who needs which visa

### 3.1 The three visitor routes

| Route | Who | Cost | Arab League nationalities |
|---|---|---|---|
| ETA (subclass 601) | 34 listed passports, among them Germany, USA, Japan, Singapore | AUD 20 app service charge | none |
| eVisitor (subclass 651) | 36 listed European passports, among them Germany | free | none |
| Visitor visa (subclass 600) | everyone else | AUD 250 | all 22 |

Both lists were read in full on the Home Affairs pages (last updated 8.9.2026). No Arab
League country is on either.

Cross-check with data (data.gov.au, visitor visas granted, to 30.6.2026): for all 22
nationalities, ETA grants = 0 and eVisitor grants = 0 in 2024-25 and in 2025-26. All
their visitor visas were subclass 600: 22,468 in 2024-25 and 20,441 in 2025-26
(my sums over the 22).

### 3.2 German citizens: confirmed, not the same visa

Germany is on both the ETA and the eVisitor list. In 2025-26 Germans received 132,593
eVisitors, 16,403 ETAs and only 2,224 subclass 600 visas. A German can apply for a
subclass 600 visa, but it is the exception.

### 3.3 Nationality-specific rules found

Nothing in the rules treats any of the 22 differently. What exists:

- **Same form and channel for everyone.** The instrument in force (LIN 21/056,
  compilation of 12.9.2025) says a Tourist stream application "must be made using form
  1419 (Internet) through ImmiAccount". A paper form is allowed only if the Department
  invites the person in writing. No nationality is paper-only.
- **Same fee**, except a lower fee for Pacific island and Timor-Leste passports since
  1.7.2026. None of the 22.
- **Frequent Traveller stream**: only for 11 Asian passports. None of the 22. It is a
  different stream and does not touch the Tourist stream.
- **Biometrics** depend on where the person applies, not on the passport. Collection
  centres are listed for 50 countries (as of March 2026), including Algeria,
  Bahrain, Egypt, Iraq, Jordan, Kuwait, Lebanon, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the UAE.
  **Germany is not on the list.** Whether an applicant in Germany is ever asked: not
  verified ("We will let you know if you need to provide biometrics").
- **The 2026 suspension power.** The Minister can now stop holders of temporary visas
  from travelling, by class, for a limited period (Migration Act s 84B, "arrival control
  determination"). Law-firm summaries say the power started on 14.3.2026 and that a
  determination lasts six months at most; the Act itself was not read. It was used once: holders of a subclass 600
  visa linked to an **Iranian** passport, 26.3.2026 to 25.9.2026. The instrument (LIN
  26/040) is marked "No longer in force". It did not stop new applications: "Applications
  already lodged, as well as new applications will continue to be assessed on their
  merits". No determination for an Arab League nationality was found. A law firm reports
  that the Department told a Senate committee how many UAE, Lebanese, Qatari, Syrian and
  Palestinian visa holders "could be caught" (secondary, not verified). **Check the
  legislation register again before the pitch**; a new determination would be a rule,
  not a bias.
- **Palestinians**: no suspension stated on the Home Affairs conflict page. Press reports
  of high refusal numbers exist (not verified here).

## 4. The real application

### 4.1 Fee

- **AUD 250** per applicant who applies from outside Australia (Migration Regulations,
  Schedule 1, item 1236(2)(a)(vi), compilation of 1.7.2026). R
- Before 1.7.2026 it was AUD 200 (migration-agent websites only, Sn).
- **Not refunded after refusal.** Home Affairs: "We do not usually refund the visa
  application charge, even if your visa application is refused or withdrawn." R
- No second instalment.
- Possible extra costs named on the visa page: "health checks", "police certificates",
  "biometrics".

### 4.2 The form (form 1419)

The live form is online-only and sits behind a login, so I could not see it. The paper
version (form 1419, design date 06/18, 17 pages) is no longer on the official site (the
link returns "401 Unauthorized"). I read a copy hosted by a German visa agency. Treat the
field list as the 2018 paper form; the online form may differ. **Current wording not
verified.**

What this means: the real form has separate fields for passport country, country of
birth, other citizenships, the country the person lives in and the legal status there.
"Nationality X, born in Germany, permanent resident of Germany" can be written in the
form's own fields.

### 4.3 Documents

From the Home Affairs page for the Tourist stream, apply outside Australia (page updated
1.7.2026). R

- **Identity**: passport pages with photo, personal details, issue and expiry dates;
  national identity card if there is one; proof of name change if any.
- **"Genuine visitor documents"**:
  - "proof you have enough money for your stay and to leave Australia", such as
    "itemised personal bank statements for a 3 month period", pay slips, audited
    accounts, tax records, term deposits, credit card statements
  - a letter of invitation from a relative or friend in Australia, if visiting one
  - "your plans while in Australia"
  - "proof that you have reasons to return home", such as "a letter from your employer
    stating you plan to return to your job", proof of study, "proof that you have
    immediate family members in your home country", "proof that you own a house or
    other major assets in your home country"
- **Character**: military service record if any; a police certificate if asked.
- **Translations**: all non-English documents, with details of the translator.
- No fixed minimum amount of money is published.
- "We may decide your application without requesting further information from you."

### 4.4 Biometrics and health

Biometrics: fingerprints and a face image, only if the Department asks; the collection
centre charges a service fee; "You may need to travel to another country". See 3.3 for
Germany. Health examinations only if asked.

### 4.5 Processing times

- All nationalities, Tourist stream, visas granted October to December 2025: a quarter
  within 4 days, half within 10 days, three quarters within 16 days (program report,
  table 4.01). R
- By nationality: section 6, last columns.
- The visa page itself refers to a processing-time tool; its numbers are loaded by
  script and I could not read them.

### 4.6 Outcome

Written decision with reasons. "Generally, we grant a stay period of 3 months", up to 12
months is possible; single or multiple entry. Review rights for a refusal from outside
Australia: migration-agent sites say there is usually none for the Tourist stream.
**Not verified in an official source.**

## 5. The legal test the officer applies

Source: Migration Regulations 1994, Compilation No. 288, 1.7.2026 (Authorised Version
F2026C00667). Quotes are exact. R

### 5.1 Criteria for the Tourist stream

**600.211, genuine temporary stay**

> The applicant genuinely intends to stay temporarily in Australia for the purpose for
> which the visa is granted, having regard to:
> (a) whether the applicant has complied substantially with the conditions to which the
> last substantive visa, or any subsequent bridging visa, held by the applicant was
> subject; and
> (b) whether the applicant intends to comply with the conditions to which the Subclass
> 600 visa would be subject; and
> (c) any other relevant matter.

**600.212, money**

> The applicant has: (a) adequate means to support himself or herself; or (b) access to
> adequate means to support himself or herself; during the period of the applicant's
> intended stay in Australia.

**600.213, public interest criteria**

> (1) The applicant satisfies public interest criteria 4001, 4002, 4003, 4004, 4005,
> 4011, 4013, 4014, 4020 and 4021.

In plain words (my labels, not checked one by one): character, security, foreign
policy, debts to the government, health, the overstay risk factor (4011), earlier
cancellations and overstays (4013, 4014), no false documents or information (4020), a
valid passport (4021).

**600.221, purpose**

> The applicant intends to visit Australia, or remain in Australia: (a) to visit an
> Australian citizen, or Australian permanent resident, who is a parent, spouse, de facto
> partner, child, brother or sister of the applicant; or (b) for any other purpose that
> is not related to business or medical treatment.

Also 600.214 (special return criteria 5001, 5002, 5010) and 600.215 (exceptional
circumstances if the stay would exceed 12 months in total).

### 5.2 Conditions on the visa (600.611)

For an unsponsored Tourist stream visa: "conditions 8101 and 8201 must be imposed" and
"conditions 8501, 8503 and 8558 may be imposed".

- 8101: "The holder must not engage in work in Australia."
- 8201: no more than 3 months of study.
- 8501: "The holder must maintain adequate arrangements for health insurance while the
  holder is in Australia."
- **8503 ("no further stay")**: "The holder will not, after entering Australia, be
  entitled to be granted a substantive visa, other than a protection visa, while the
  holder remains in Australia."
- 8558: "The holder must not stay in Australia for more than 12 months in any period of
  18 months."

(The condition numbers were scrambled in the PDF text; I matched 8501, 8503 and 8558 to
their wording by position. 8101 is certain.)

8503 is discretionary here, so it could serve as an extra, realistic outcome: the
officer decides whether to add it. Policy says officers "may consider imposing an 8503
if they have residual concerns about the applicant's intentions, but this should not be
a default setting".

### 5.3 The nationality clause: public interest criterion 4011

Still in the law on 1.7.2026:

> 4011 (1) If the applicant is affected by the risk factor specified in subclause (2),
> the applicant satisfies the Minister that, having regard to the applicant's
> circumstances in the applicant's country of usual residence, there is very little
> likelihood that the applicant will remain after the expiry of any period during which
> the applicant might be authorised to remain after entry.
> (2) An applicant is affected by the risk factor referred to in subclause (1) if:
> (a) during the period of 5 years immediately preceding the application, the applicant
> has applied for a visa for the purpose of permanent residence in Australia; or
> (b) the applicant has all the characteristics of a class of persons specified in a
> legislative instrument made by the Minister for the purposes of this paragraph.
> (2A) In specifying a class of persons for the purposes of paragraph (2)(b), the
> Minister must have regard to statistics prepared by the Secretary: (a) from movement
> records kept by Immigration about persons who have remained in Australia after expiry
> of the period during which each person was authorised to remain [...]
> (3) For the purposes of paragraph (2)(b), a characteristic is any of the following:
> (a) nationality; (b) marital or relationship status; (c) age; (d) sex; (e) occupation;
> (f) the class of visa currently applied for; (g) the place of lodgment or posting of
> the application for that visa.

So the law allows a list of risk groups defined by nationality, built from overstay
statistics. Two facts about the list:

- **The last list (IMMI 08/033, in force 13.6.2008 to 14.5.2009)** had 98 groups, each a
  nationality with sex and age band. From the Arab League: Egypt, Iraq, Jordan, Lebanon
  and Syria (for example "Lebanon Female 20 years or older", "Lebanon Male 20 years or
  older", "Syria Male 25 to 39 years inclusive"). Also on it: Turkey, Brazil, Chile,
  Israel, the Philippines, Vietnam, Russia, Ukraine. Not on it: any Gulf state, Morocco,
  Tunisia, Algeria.
- **It was revoked from 15.5.2009 (IMMI 08/119).** The explanatory statement: "The
  'Risk Factor List' has been criticised as an inflexible tool. [...] the Gazette Notice
  made under paragraph 4011(2)(b) will be replaced by evidence based profiles maintained
  in the Safeguards system. These profiles will be updated regularly and assist
  departmental officers to make informed decision by providing relevant information
  about the social and political situation in the applicant's home country and
  immigration non-compliance trends in the visitor visa caseload."

I found no later list. **Not verified exhaustively** (the register's search did not work
for me); the two instruments above were read in full.

Note the wording of 4011(1): "circumstances in the applicant's **country of usual
residence**". For the planned applicants that is Germany, not the passport country.

### 5.4 What published policy lets the officer consider

Source: the Department's instruction "GenGuideH - Visitor visas - Visa application and
related procedures", reissued 10.9.2016, released under FOI in 2018 (FA 18/04/00125).
It may have been replaced since; the current internal instruction is not public.
**Current wording not verified.** I read the sections on genuine temporary stay, on
criterion 4011 and on means of support. R

Under "any other relevant matter" (600.211(c)) it lists: "Personal circumstances",
"Credibility", "Purpose and period of stay", "Previous immigration/travel history",
"Intel reports and profile".

**Home country means where the person lives:**

> the personal circumstances of the applicant that would encourage them to return to
> their home country (country of usual residence) at the end of the proposed visit, such
> as: ongoing employment; the presence of close family members in their home country
> [...]; property, or other significant assets, owned in their home country and
> **whether the applicant is currently residing in a country whose nationals represent a
> low risk of immigration non-compliance, even if the applicant is originally from a
> country whose nationals represent a statistically higher risk of non-compliance**

**Conditions in the home country count:**

> the personal circumstances of the applicant in their home country or general
> conditions in the home country that might encourage them to remain in Australia, such
> as: economic circumstances [...]; economic disruption, including shortages, famine, or
> high levels of unemployment, or natural disasters in the applicant's home country
> [...]; military service commitments; civil disruption, including war, lawlessness or
> political upheaval in the applicant's home country.

**Statistics by nationality may be used, but only to look closer:**

> Intel reports and profiles: Information in statistical, intelligence and analysis
> reports on migration fraud and immigration compliance compiled by the department about
> nationals from the applicant's home country.

> Officers may request further evidence from the applicant, if considered appropriate,
> if departmental statistical or intelligence reports on migration fraud, or profiles
> based on such reports, indicate that there is a significantly greater likelihood of
> nationals from the applicant's home country: staying in Australia beyond the stay
> period of their visa or having their visa cancelled or being refused entry to
> Australia or making asylum claims or applying for a protection visa.

> Note: The mere fact that an applicant matches the characteristics of a profile is not
> grounds to refuse to grant a visa. Profiles are merely an alert that closer scrutiny of
> the applicant's circumstances might be required. All applications must be considered
> on their own merits taking into account all the information and supporting
> documentation provided by the applicant.

On criterion 4011: "Applicants must not, however, be refused a visa solely on the basis
that they match the risk factor characteristics." And: if the officer is satisfied that
the intention to visit is genuine and all other requirements are met, "the visa must be
granted (s65 of the Act refers)".

The old overstay statistic by nationality ("Modified Non Return Rate") "should not be
used because no quarterly MNRR report has been published on the departrment's website
since June 2013" (typo in the original).

**Summary in plain words.** The rule is individual: does this person intend a temporary
stay, and can they pay for it. Nationality is not a ground for refusal. But official
policy says conditions in the country where the person lives count, statistics about
"nationals from the applicant's home country" may trigger closer checks, and living in a
low-risk country counts in the applicant's favour even with a higher-risk passport. For
a file that says "born in Germany, lives in Germany, employed there", the published
policy points towards treating all 22 passports alike.

**Caution.** These are real applicants. Most live in their home country, with very
different incomes, jobs and reasons to return. The numbers do not show what an officer
does with identical files, and they do not show bias. They are a yardstick for
comparison only, in the same way the EU list was in the bank design. Small countries
(Comoros, Djibouti, Mauritania, Somalia, Libya) have too few cases for a stable rate.

## 10. Points for the design (observations, not decisions)

- **Residence in Germany is what the rules look at.** Criterion 4011 and the policy both
  speak of the country of usual residence. The policy sentence about "currently residing
  in a country whose nationals represent a low risk" says in so many words that such an
  applicant should not be treated like someone living in the passport country. A model
  that still rates the 22 files differently departs from the published policy.
- **That policy sentence could be the "second version" paragraph**, parallel to the
  nationality paragraph in the bank design: once without it, once with it. No new factor
  is needed for that.
- **The form asks for the place of birth.** Leaving it out is less realistic than in
  the bank file; stating "Germany" for everyone keeps it constant.
- **A yardstick exists, but it is old and rough**: real grant rates by citizenship for
  2019-20, and pooled refusal rates for 2023-24. Unlike the EU list it is a scale, not a
  yes or no.
- **German benchmark does not fit.** Turkish is the natural benchmark for Germany.
- **Realism caution**: people who were born and raised in Germany and hold, say, an
  Emirati, Qatari or Omani passport are rare. This is my inference, not a sourced fact.
- **Risk to watch**: a new arrival control determination naming an Arab League
  nationality (3.3), and the fee, which changed on 1.7.2026.
